01

Getting started

Evopix is a full photo editor that runs on your phone. You can edit photos, work in layers, cut out parts of a picture, add text and shapes, fix colours and export the result. There is no account to create and nothing is uploaded: every photo stays on your phone.

Time5 minutes
LevelComplete beginner
You needAn Android phone or tablet

This page is the full manual. Read it from start to finish, or use the tabs at the top to jump to the part you need. Everything in the app is explained in the same order you will meet it.

The first time you open Evopix

  1. Open the app. Evopix starts in landscape (the phone held sideways). The editor has many tools, and a wide screen gives them room and keeps your picture large.
  2. Read the small welcome card. The first time only, a card says "Use this app in landscape mode. You will enjoy it. Thanks!" Tap Got it. It never appears again.
  3. Allow photo access when asked. Android asks once. Evopix needs it only to show your gallery so you can pick a picture. Nothing is copied or sent anywhere.
  4. You land on Home. This is the list of your projects. The first tile is Create New.
Good to knowIf you prefer holding the phone upright, tap the rotate button beside Menu in the editor, or switch on Auto-rotate in Settings. Both are explained in the Settings chapter.

Your first edit, step by step

This short exercise touches the main parts of the app. It takes about five minutes and you can undo anything.

  1. Tap Create New, then Photo library, and tap any picture. It opens in the editor.
  2. Learn to move around: pinch with two fingers to zoom, and drag with two fingers to slide the picture. Drag with one finger does nothing by default, so you can never move the picture by accident.
  3. Tap Adjust in the top bar. Move Brightness a little to the right. The picture changes live while you drag. Tap Apply to keep it, or Cancel to leave without changing anything.
  4. Tap Filters and choose Warm. Move Strength until it looks natural, then Apply.
  5. Pick the Text tool, tap on the picture, type a word, choose a font and tap Apply. Your word is now its own layer.
  6. Tap the Undo arrow at the top right a few times to see your steps disappear, then Redo to bring them back.
  7. Tap Export (the arrow icon at the top right), choose JPG, and tap Save…. Your finished picture is now in your gallery.
TipYou never need to press Save to keep your work. Evopix saves the project by itself, so you can close the app and continue later from Home. You can change this in Settings.

How the app is organised

Home
Your projects, templates and settings. Everything starts here.
Editor
The workspace. Tools on the left, layers on the right, panels at the top, settings for the current tool at the bottom.
Panels
Adjust, Correct, Grade, Filters and Crop open as panels. Each has live preview and Cancel / Apply, so nothing changes until you press Apply.
Layers
Every piece of your design lives on its own layer, so you can always change one thing without touching the rest.

Making a blank canvas

Good to knowBigger canvases give sharper results but use more memory. For social posts, 1080 pixels on the long side is plenty. For print, use larger sizes.

Using a template

  1. Tap Templates on the Home screen.
  2. Tap a group name such as Poster to show only that kind, or stay on All.
  3. Tap a template. It opens as a new project, so the original template is never changed.
  4. Change the words: open the Layers panel, tap the text layer, then tap ✎ Edit text. You can change the words, font, colour and size.
  5. Add your photo: tap Import at the bottom, choose Gallery, and pick the picture. It arrives as a new layer on top.
  6. Place the photo: use Transform to move and resize it. Use Move down in the Layers panel if it covers the text.
  7. Export when you are happy.
Try itOpen Flash Sale, change the headline to your shop name, add your product photo with Import, send the photo layer below the text, and export as PNG.
TipTemplates are made of separate layers. Hide a layer with its eye icon to see what is underneath, or delete any piece you do not want. Nothing else moves.
Good to knowIf a template font does not suit you, select the text layer and pick a different font, or import your own in Settings → Fonts.

Zoom, move and undo

Before you edit, you need to move comfortably around the picture. These controls work everywhere in the editor.

WhereAnywhere in the editor
GesturesPinch, two-finger drag

Zoom

  • Pinch with two fingers to zoom in and out smoothly.
  • Tap the round magnifier button for exact controls: +, −, fit to screen and 100%.
  • Fit shows the whole picture. 100% shows real pixels, which is best for checking sharpness and retouching.

Move the view

  • Two-finger drag always slides the picture around, whatever tool is active.
  • Or choose Pan in the bottom bar. Then a single finger slides the picture. Switch back to a normal tool afterwards.
TipZoom in close before painting, selecting or healing. A bigger view means more precise edits, and you can zoom out afterwards to judge the result.

Undo and redo

The two arrows at the top right step backward and forward through your changes. Every apply, stroke, text and layer action is a step.

Undo
Takes back the last step. Tap repeatedly to go further back.
Redo
Brings back a step you undid. It is cleared as soon as you make a new edit.
How many steps?
Set in Settings → Saving and export → Undo steps (5 to 50).
Good to knowMore undo steps use more memory, especially with large pictures. If the app feels slow, lower the number.

Mouse and keyboard

If you use a tablet with a mouse or keyboard, extra shortcuts exist. Open Settings → Mouse & keyboard → Keyboard & mouse shortcuts to see them all. For example [ and ] make the brush smaller and bigger.

Layers

A layer is like a sheet of clear plastic laid over your picture. Each sheet holds one thing: a photo, a word, a shape, a brush stroke. Because they are separate, you can move, hide, recolour or delete one sheet without disturbing anything else. Almost every good edit uses layers.

WhereRight column, and the Layers panel
Why use themEdit one thing safely

Why layers matter

Imagine you paint on the photo directly and later decide the paint was a mistake. You can only undo. If you paint on a separate layer, you can simply hide or delete that layer later, even after many other edits. Separate layers give you freedom.

The basics

  1. Tap New empty layer (bottom right) to add a blank, transparent layer on top.
  2. Tap a layer preview in the right column to make it the active layer. Everything you paint, fill, erase, blur or adjust now affects only that layer.
  3. Open the Layers panel for the full list of controls for that layer.
Good to knowThe active layer is highlighted. If a tool seems to do nothing, check you have the right layer selected, or that it is not hidden or locked.

What you can do to a layer

＋ Empty layer
Adds a blank layer above the active one.
Import image
Brings a picture in as a new layer.
Select pixels
Makes a selection of everything the layer contains, useful to move or cut it.
✎ Edit text
Shown for text layers. Change the words, font and style at any time.
✎ Rename
Give the layer a clear name, for example "Logo".
Duplicate
Makes an exact copy. Great for trying an effect without risk.
Delete
Removes the layer.
Merge down
Joins this layer into the one below it, making them a single layer. You cannot split them again, so use with care.
▲ Move up / ▼ Move down
Changes stacking. Layers higher in the list appear in front.
Lock layer
Prevents accidental changes. Tap again to unlock.
Visible / Hidden
Show or hide the layer without deleting it.
Opacity
How see-through the layer is, from 0 (invisible) to 100% (solid).
Blend mode
How the layer mixes with those below.

Blend modes in plain words

Blend modes decide how the colours of a layer combine with what is underneath. Try them one by one on a layer and watch the picture.

Normal
The layer simply covers what is below it.
Multiply
Makes things darker, like stacking transparent film. Good for shadows, textures and paper effects.
Screen
Makes things lighter, the opposite of Multiply. Black disappears. Great for light leaks, glows and flares.
Overlay
Adds contrast and richness while keeping both layers visible. Great for textures and colour grading.
Darken
Keeps whichever colour is darker.
Lighten
Keeps whichever colour is lighter.
Add
Adds the light of both together. Very bright. Best for fire, sparks and strong glows.
Try itImport a flare from the App library, set its Blend Mode to Screen, then lower Opacity to about 70%. The black background vanishes and only the glow remains.
TipName your layers and keep important ones locked. In a template or a big design you will thank yourself later.

Selections and the Magic Wand

A selection is a moving dotted outline around part of your picture. While it is active, whatever you do (paint, fill, adjust, delete, copy) happens only inside it. Selections are how you change a sky without touching a building, or brighten a face without brightening the background.

WhereSelect panel, or the selection tools on the left
Best forLocal edits, cut-outs, sky changes

The selection tools

Box
Drag to draw a rectangle. Fast for straight-edged areas.
Lasso
Draw any shape with your finger and lift to close it. Best for free-form areas.
Curve lasso
Tap points to build a smooth curved outline. Best for round objects.
Magic wand
Tap a colour and it selects all similar colours touching that spot. Best for skies, walls and backgrounds.
Snap
Helps outlines stick to edges and the grid. Turn it on for neat shapes.

Using the Magic Wand

  1. Choose Wand in the tool column.
  2. Tap the area you want. The outline appears.
  3. Without lifting your finger, drag left or right. The selection grows or shrinks live. This is the Tolerance: how different a colour can be and still be included. Lift when it looks right.
  4. Use Wand mode in the bottom bar to build up the selection in steps (below).

Low tolerance picks only almost identical colours, so selections are small and exact. High tolerance picks many similar colours and spreads wide. For a plain wall use a low value; for a sky with a gradient use a higher one.

Wand modes

New
Starts a fresh selection every tap.
Add
Adds the new area to what is already selected. Use when the wand missed a bit.
Subtract
Removes the new area from the selection. Use when the wand grabbed too much.
Intersect
Keeps only the part where the old and new selections overlap.

Wand advanced options

Follow gradients
The wand follows smooth colour changes instead of stopping at a fixed tolerance. Excellent for skies and soft shadows.
Reads: all layers / active layer
Choose whether the wand looks at the layer you are on, or at everything you can see.
Edge stop
Makes the wand stop at weak edges. Higher numbers stop sooner. 0 is off.
Smooth edges
Softens the outline so it is less jagged.
Touching only / All similar areas
Select just the connected area, or every similar spot in the whole picture.

Refine a selection

In the Select panel, under Refine selection, set an amount in pixels and tap one of these:

Grow
Makes the selection bigger by that many pixels. Helps remove a thin fringe left at the edge.
Shrink
Makes it smaller.
Smooth outline
Rounds off rough, jagged edges.
Fill holes
Fills small gaps inside the selection.
Remove specks
Clears tiny stray dots outside the main area.

Selection actions

Select all
Selects the entire picture.
Deselect
Removes the selection.
Invert
Swaps selected and unselected. Select the sky, then Invert to select everything except the sky.
Copy / Cut
Copies or cuts the selected pixels.
Paste as layer
Pastes them as a new layer you can move.
Fill with color
Fills the selection with your paint colour.
Delete
Erases the selected pixels on the active layer.
Crop to it
Crops the whole picture down to the selection.
Try itCut out a person: Wand the plain background, tap Invert, then Shrink by 1 px and Smooth outline. Use Cut then Paste as layer. The person is now on their own layer.
TipZoom in while selecting. If something goes wrong, Undo restores the selection too.
08

Painting and retouching

These tools let you draw on a layer, clean up a picture and blend colours. Always paint on a separate layer when you can, so the original stays safe.

WhereTool column (left)
TipPick the right layer first

Brush and eraser

  1. Choose Brush in the tool column.
  2. Tap Color in the bottom bar. Pick a colour, and save colours you use often.
  3. Tap Brush to set softness. A hard brush has a crisp edge; a soft brush fades gently and looks more natural.
  4. Draw on the picture. Choose Eraser to rub out. Eraser works only on the active layer.
Good to knowBrush size and opacity start from the values in Settings → Brush defaults. With a keyboard, [ and ] change the size.
Edge Aware
The brush stays inside an area of similar colour so you can paint right up to an edge without crossing it. Great for colouring objects neatly.
Stylus
If you draw with a pen, turn this on so pressing harder paints thicker or stronger. Strength is set in Settings → Brush defaults.

Fill (paint bucket)

Choose Fill and tap an area. It fills the connected area of similar colour with your paint colour. Change Tolerance to fill a wider or narrower range. With an active selection, it fills only inside the selection.

Colour picker

Choose Picker and tap anywhere on the picture. That exact colour becomes your paint colour. Use it to match a colour from the photo, such as the skin tone or a sunset.

Clone stamp

  1. Choose Clone.
  2. Set the source spot you want to copy from, following the on-screen hint.
  3. Paint over the place you want to cover. The pixels from your source are copied there.
TipPick a source spot with similar light and texture. Use a soft brush and small strokes so the result does not look repeated.

Heal

Choose Heal and paint over a spot, scratch or blemish. Evopix replaces it using the nearby texture and blends the edges. Best for small things like pimples, dust and tiny objects.

Gradient

  1. Choose Gradient. The Gradient panel opens.
  2. Pick a Type: Linear (straight blend), Radial (from a centre outward), Angle (around a point) or Reflected (mirrored on both sides).
  3. Pick a Preset such as Sunset, Ocean, Fire, Aurora, Midnight, Mint or Rainbow, or choose Foreground → Background, Black → White and similar.
  4. Tap a colour dot to change it. Press and hold a colour to remove it.
  5. Drag across the picture to set where the blend starts and ends, then tap Apply.
  6. Tap Save gradient to keep your own for later.
Try itMake a new layer, apply a Foreground → Transparent gradient from the bottom edge upward in black, then set the layer to Multiply. You now have a soft shadow at the bottom for text to sit on.
09

Adding text

Text turns a photo into a post, poster or card. Every piece of text is its own layer, so you can change it at any time.

WhereText tool (left)
Best forCaptions, titles, quotes

Add text

  1. Choose the Text tool, then tap on the picture. The Add text panel opens.
  2. Type your words in the box.
  3. Choose a Font: Sans, Serif, Mono, Script, Poppins and more. Tap All fonts… to see everything including fonts you imported.
  4. Set the Style: bold, italic, alignment, size and letter spacing.
  5. Pick the text colour.
  6. Switch on Outline to draw a border around the letters. Choose its width and colour.
  7. Switch on Shadow to add a shadow. Choose blur, distance, angle and opacity.
  8. Tap Apply. The text becomes a new layer.

Choosing a good look

Outline
Helps text stay readable over busy photos. A dark outline on white text works almost anywhere.
Shadow
Adds depth and lifts text off the picture. Keep it soft: low distance, medium blur.
Letter spacing
Wider spacing feels calm and premium, good for titles. Tight spacing feels strong, good for big words.
Alignment
Left, centre or right inside the text box. Centre suits posters and quotes.

Change text later

  1. Open the Layers panel.
  2. Select the text layer.
  3. Tap ✎ Edit text. The same panel opens with your words.
  4. Make your changes and tap Apply.

Move, resize and rotate text

Select the text layer and use Transform in the bottom bar to move, scale and rotate it. You can also change its opacity and blend mode like any layer.

Use your own fonts

Download a .ttf or .otf font file, then open Settings → Fonts → ＋ Import font… and choose the file. It will appear in the font list.

TipFor readable text on a photo: add a soft dark gradient behind it, or use outline plus shadow. Never use thin fonts over detail.
10

Shapes

Shapes help build buttons, frames, badges, dividers and decoration. Each shape you draw lands on its own layer.

WhereShapes tool (left)
Best forFrames, badges, buttons, icons

Draw a shape

  1. Choose Shapes in the tool column.
  2. Pick a shape: Rectangle, Square, Rounded, Circle, Oval, Triangle, Line, Arrow, Star, Polygon or Heart.
  3. Pick a style: Fill (solid), Outline (border only) or Fill + outline. Fill uses your paint colour and the outline uses your background colour.
  4. Adjust Outline / line width, Rounded corner radius, and Star points / polygon sides if needed.
  5. Drag on the picture to draw it.
Rounded
A rectangle with soft corners. Use the radius slider to make it more or less round.
Star
Set the number of points. Five is a classic star, more points makes a sunburst.
Polygon
Set the number of sides. 6 makes a hexagon, 8 an octagon.
Line / Arrow
Drag from start to end. Use line width for thickness.

Edit a shape later

Select the shape layer and use Transform to move, scale or rotate it. Change opacity and blend mode in the Layers panel. To change its colour, repaint with Fill while the layer is active.

TipTurn on Snap or Grid snap to make shapes line up. Combine a rounded rectangle with text for a button, or a circle with a photo for a profile picture.
11

Adjust, Correct and Grade

These three panels change how your colours look. Each one gives live preview, so you see the result while you drag. Nothing is permanent until you tap Apply.

WhereTop bar: Adjust, Correct, Grade
Always availableReset · Cancel · Apply

Which panel should you use? Adjust for quick fixes, Correct for careful control, Grade for a ready-made mood.

Adjust: the quick basics

Brightness
Makes the whole picture lighter or darker.
Contrast
Increases or reduces the difference between light and dark. Higher contrast looks punchier.
Saturation
How vivid the colours are. Zero is black and white.
Hue
Rotates all colours around the colour wheel. Use gently or colours look unreal.
Red / Green / Blue
Adds or removes one colour channel. Good for fixing a colour cast.
Temperature
Cooler (blue) to warmer (orange).
Tint
Greener to more magenta.

Correct: finer control

Light
Exposure, Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks. Use Highlights to recover bright skies, Shadows to lift dark faces.
Colour
Temperature, Tint, Vibrance and Saturation. Vibrance boosts dull colours more than already strong ones, so skin stays natural.
Colour wheels
Three wheels for Shadows, Midtones and Highlights. Drag the dot toward a colour to tint that part of the picture. Hold still to reset.
Curves
Master, Red, Green and Blue curves for precise control of light and colour.

Using the colour wheels

  1. Open Correct and choose the Shadows, Midtones or Highlights tab.
  2. Drag the dot from the centre toward a colour. The further you go, the stronger the tint.
  3. A popular look: teal in the shadows and orange in the highlights, which gives a film-like feel.
  4. Hold your finger still on the wheel to reset it.

Grade: looks (LUTs)

A LUT is a ready-made colour look, like a film preset. Pick one and the whole picture takes on that mood.

  1. Tap Grade in the top bar.
  2. Choose a Built-in look, or browse the LUT packs by category.
  3. Move Strength to blend the look in or out. 100% is full effect; around 50 to 70% is often more natural.
  4. Tap Apply.

To use your own looks, tap ＋ Import .cube file. They then show under Your LUTs. Press and hold one to delete it.

TipIn portrait mode these panels open as a short sheet at the bottom, so your picture stays visible above them.
Try itFix a flat photo: Adjust → Contrast up a little → Correct → raise Highlights down and Shadows up → Grade → choose a look at 60% strength.

Filters, effects, blur and sky replacement

Filters change the whole look of a layer quickly. Open Filters in the top bar, choose one, adjust its settings, and tap Apply. Everything has live preview.

WhereTop bar: Filters; Blur in the bottom bar
Best forFast mood, special effects

Sky replacement

  1. Open Filters and find the Sky replacement group.
  2. Tap a sky: Blue day, Clear sky, Fluffy clouds, Sunset, Golden hour, Dawn, Dusk, Starry night, Stormy or Pastel.
  3. Adjust the settings that appear and tap Apply.
Good to knowSky replacement works best when the original sky is fairly clear and the horizon has strong edges. Bright white buildings can be mistaken for sky.

Colour filters

Invert
Flips every colour to its opposite.
Black & White
Removes all colour.
Sepia
Warm brown, old-photo tone.
Warm / Cool
Shifts the picture toward orange or blue.
Vintage
Faded colours with an older feel.
High contrast
Strong dark and light.
Fade
Lifts the blacks for a soft, matte look.

Each has a Strength slider. Many filters look best at half strength.

Effects and style

Glass
A frosted glass look.
Stroke
Adds an outline around the content of a layer.
Shadow
Adds a drop shadow behind it.
Glow
Adds a soft light around the edges.
RGB split
Offsets the red, green and blue channels for a glitch look.
Grain
Adds film grain, which also helps hide banding.
Sharpen
Makes details crisper. Use lightly.
Pixelate
Turns the picture into big square pixels.
Vignette
Darkens the corners to focus attention on the centre.

Blur types

Tap Blur in the bottom bar to blur the active layer. Select an area first to blur only that part.

Gaussian
Smooth, natural blur. The everyday choice.
Box
A simple even blur.
Motion
Looks like movement in one direction.
Zoom
Streaks outward from the centre, like speed.
Spin
Swirls around a centre point.
Tilt-shift
Keeps a stripe sharp and blurs the rest so the scene looks like a tiny model.
Try itMake a soft background: duplicate the photo layer, apply Gaussian blur to the lower copy, then use Eraser on the upper copy to reveal the sharp subject.
13

Crop, rotate and resize

Use these to frame your picture better, straighten it and set the exact size.

WhereTop bar: Crop; bottom bar: Transform
Best forFraming, straightening, resizing

Transform one layer

  • Rotate: turns the layer from −180° to +180°. Use small angles to straighten a crooked horizon.
  • Scale: makes it smaller or bigger.
  • You can also drag to move the layer to the exact place you want.

Change the whole image

Saving and exporting

Exporting makes a normal picture file you can share. Saving a project keeps every layer so you can keep editing.

WhereTop bar: Export icon
FormatsPNG and JPG

Export a finished picture

  1. Tap the Export icon at the top right (or Export PNG / JPG… in the ⋮ menu).
  2. Choose a Format: PNG or JPG (explained below).
  3. For JPG, set the JPG quality.
  4. Set Width and Height if you want a different size. Keep Keep proportions on.
  5. Tap Save… and choose where the file goes.
PNG
Best quality. Keeps transparency. Larger files. Use for logos, graphics and anything with see-through parts.
JPG
Smaller files and good for photos. No transparency. A quality of 85 to 92% looks great and stays small.

Save a project you can edit later

Your project is already saved inside the app. To back it up or move it to another phone, use ⋮ → Save copy as .zip (keeps layers). To open it later, tap the folder icon on Home, or use Open .zip project.

CarefulRemoving the app deletes the projects stored inside it. Save a .zip copy of important work somewhere safe, such as your cloud storage.

Questions and answers

Short answers to the questions people ask most.

Still stuck?Email us from the last page
Why does the app open in landscape?

Evopix is designed for landscape because the tools fit best. Tap the rotate button next to Menu to switch, or turn on Auto-rotate in Settings.

Why does Evopix ask for photo access?

Only to show your gallery so you can choose pictures. Your photos stay on your phone and are never uploaded.

Does Evopix need internet?

No. Everything, including the App library, works offline.

I lost my project. Can I get it back?

Projects are stored inside the app. If the app was removed or its data cleared, they are gone. Save a .zip backup of anything important.

Why is my exported JPG not transparent?

JPG cannot hold transparency. Export as PNG.

The app feels slow with a big picture.

Lower Undo steps in Settings, close other apps, or work at a smaller canvas size.

How do I get the old layout back?

Settings → Editor layout → Original UI, or use ⋮ → Layout: switch in the editor.

The wand selected too much or too little.

Drag left to reduce tolerance or right to increase it. Use Add and Subtract modes to fix small mistakes.

My text is hard to read.

Add an outline and a soft shadow, or place a dark gradient layer behind it.

Where do I report a problem?

Email evopixsupport@gmail.com with your phone model and what you were doing.
